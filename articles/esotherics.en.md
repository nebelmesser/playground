I've been reading Threads and noticed that hardline believers in science and esoteric thinkers have more in common than they might think. Both tend to:
1. Know each other mainly through memes they barely understand.
2. Bring their maps to the wrong territory.
3. Want one language that explains everything, and struggle with a view of the world that stays incomplete and uncertain.

How do I get you two to make peace?
Below: a look at where each approach belongs, with examples even tech people can follow ↓

To reduce the misunderstanding, we first need to see the difference in approach:
- Science organizes recurring, reproducible phenomena that tend to be independent of the observer.
- Esotericism organizes a person's unique inner experience, which cannot be measured from the outside.

The first asks, “What is it, and how does it work?” The second asks, “What is it like, and what does it resemble?”
I'm using “esotericism” as an example here. You could put “religion” or “art” in its place.
For tech people: hardware and software. One is about how the computer is built, the other about the state it is in. Software is constrained by hardware, but it cannot be reduced to hardware. Knowing the hardware helps when writing software, but it isn't strictly necessary.

Science is strong where an event can be repeated, measured, and compared between observers. It needs stable patterns: what will happen again under the same conditions.
Esoteric systems have historically worked with different material: a particular person's unique experience. A dream, a sense of meaning, a strange coincidence, an inner turning point. An event like that can be the most important thing in your life and still be completely useless as a scientific experiment.

Esoteric thinkers fight an imaginary Science that supposedly forbids them to feel, believe, see symbols, or live at all. “It refuses to recognize the phenomenon!” Meanwhile, science quietly gets on with its work and forbids no such thing.
Science is more modest than its fans. It has plenty of fundamental limits: chaos, statistical uncertainty, incomplete models. It can say, “We don't know here,” and, “Perhaps we can't know in this form.” And you don't have to believe in science to use what it produces.

“Cluck, cluck,” says the science zealot, “but your esotericism isn't scientific!”
Neither is *The Lord of the Rings*. It has orcs, magic rings, and immortal elves. Yet for an ordinary person thinking about power, fear, duty, loss, and temptation, it may be more useful than all of quantum mechanics.
Scientific validity asks how well a model describes the outside world. Usefulness is a different question. Sometimes you need to understand why you've spent ten years unable to throw away your own Ring, rather than understand an electron's wave function.

The reductionist's favorite tool is analysis: take something apart, measure it, find the causes. It works wonderfully while the question is about how a mechanism works.
Then, out of habit, they carry the scalpel further and try to dissect love, meaning, beauty, or subjective experience, as though listing every part should make the whole disappear.
“Consciousness is just electrical impulses.” Physically true at one level of description, and useless at another.

The esoteric thinker has the opposite obsession: synthesis. They are good at bringing scattered experiences together into images, symbols, and connections. Then they start combining absolutely everything.
Tarot + chakras + quantum physics + Jung + astrology suddenly become pieces of one Great System that explains life, the universe, and everything, and is available as a twelve-module course.
Synthesis helps while it lets us see the whole. At some point, the whole gets too big, and the map starts swallowing the territory.

Unfortunately, evolution has left us with a habit of finding too much structure in the world.
Apes that mistook a snake for a stick, failing to see the pattern, got eaten by the snake. Those that mistook a stick for a snake just jumped and worried one extra time. They survived to have descendants, who now post on Threads. Finding patterns is vital to survival, so it pays to overdo it a little :)
For tech people: we tend to favor false positives over false negatives.

Both sides struggle with an incomplete picture of the world and look at the same fog from opposite shores.
The esoteric thinker sees a scheme behind the fog: levels, energies, archetypes. Read the signs correctly and the world will make sense. They dream up a SYSTEM and stretch it over every phenomenon. QUANTUM SPIRAL DYNAMICS!
The hardline science believer trusts a mirror version: the fog is temporary; we just need more data and better instruments. Eventually, we'll be able to describe everything, and then life will be great. Until then, keep your witchcraft away from us. It's unscientific!

“Cluck, cluck,” says the science zealot, “we just haven't measured the brain in enough detail yet!”
Imagine a scientist from the mid-twentieth century given a freight car full of smartphones. They can open them, measure voltages and frequencies, map the components, and find out which parts become active when different functions run.
But the concepts of “operating system,” “process,” “file,” and “app” won't simply fall out of hardware measurements. We don't know at which level the answer lies, either. That's why the problem of consciousness is hard.

“So what should we do?” you ask. “Where do we go from here?”
